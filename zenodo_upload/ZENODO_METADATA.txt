TITLE
AlienX 3D: A General SO(3)-Equivariant Neural Operator for 3D Physical Fields

CREATORS
Yaka, Eric Heylel Danjuma (Elbàlor / The Digital Necromancer)
Affiliation: Capital Software / Next Gen Tech, Abuja, Nigeria

DESCRIPTION
AlienX 3D is a 421,507-parameter neural operator that learns the evolution of
3D physical fields across multiple physical regimes without retraining. It is
built from four PDE-agnostic mechanisms: (1) an SO(3)-equivariant local frame
derived from the gradient and Hessian of a scalar field, (2) Quantum
Self-Attention (QSA) — complex-valued attention in CP^(n-1) with a geometric
phase, (3) per-sample non-dimensionalization with regime conditioning, and
(4) Biot–Savart feedback that enforces divergence-free rollouts by
construction.

Demonstrated on incompressible Navier-Stokes (Taylor-Green vortex, G=32):
0.46% single-step relative RMSE across three Reynolds numbers (300, 628,
1200) in one model; QSA ablation degrades validation MSE by 32.81x; stable
73-step autoregressive rollout (24x the training horizon) with energy error
bounded under 6%.

Zero-shot generalization: on a held-out Reynolds number (Re=900, never seen
in training) the model achieves 0.53% single-step relative RMSE, all rollout
invariants land on the interpolation trend between the trained regimes, and
ablating the complex phase channel (gi=0) degrades accuracy 4.74x — evidence
the model interpolates the physics of unseen regimes rather than memorizing
trajectories.

The release contains the trained checkpoint, normalization statistics,
importable model and physics code, the full zero-shot evaluation harness
(with a sanity gate that reproduces the in-distribution paper number before
emitting any new number), the figure-generation pipeline, all result metrics,
three paper figures (300 DPI), the complete paper (PDF + editable source),
and the full 15-bug debugging log (Section 8). Every number in the paper is
regenerable from the included code on a single 4 GB GPU.

NOTES: The released checkpoint is the epoch-55 K=3 stage of the K=1->2->3->5
curriculum (the K=5 stage was cut short by a Colab session timeout; disclosed
and analyzed in the paper, Sections 5.2 and 7.4).
